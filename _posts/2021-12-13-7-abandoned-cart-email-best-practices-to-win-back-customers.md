Cart abandonment sits at roughly 70% across ecommerce — most shoppers who add an item never complete checkout. A well-timed email series recovers a meaningful slice of that lost revenue without spending a cent on new ad traffic. Here's what actually works.

## 1. Send the First Email Within an Hour

Waiting a day or more kills your recovery rate — by then the shopper's moved on or bought elsewhere. The first reminder should land within 1 hour of abandonment, while the product (and the intent to buy) is still fresh.

## 2. Show the Exact Item They Left Behind

A generic "you left something in your cart" email underperforms one that shows the product image, name and price. Buyers respond to seeing exactly what they were about to buy.

## 3. Lead with a Clear, Single CTA

One button — "Complete Your Order" — that drops them straight back into their cart. Every extra link or offer competing for attention lowers your click-through.

## 4. Build a 3-Email Sequence, Not Just One

- **Email 1 (1 hour later):** Simple reminder, no discount.
- **Email 2 (24 hours later):** Address common objections — free shipping threshold, return policy, stock urgency.
- **Email 3 (48-72 hours later):** A small incentive (5-10% off or free shipping) for shoppers who still haven't converted.

## 5. Create Real Urgency When It's True

"Only 3 left in stock" or "price goes up after today" works — but only when it's accurate. Sellers who fake urgency see it backfire on trust and future opens.

## 6. Make Checkout Frictionless on Mobile

A large share of abandoned-cart emails get opened on a phone. If the checkout flow isn't fast and thumb-friendly, you lose the recovered click a second time.

## 7. Track Recovery Rate, Not Just Opens

The metric that matters is completed orders from the sequence, not open rate. Benchmark against your own baseline and tighten the sequence (timing, subject lines, incentive size) based on what actually converts.
