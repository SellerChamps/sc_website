## What Retail Arbitrage Actually Is

Retail arbitrage means buying discounted or clearance items from physical retail stores and reselling them on Amazon through FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) for a profit. It's one of the lowest-barrier ways to start selling on Amazon — no manufacturing, no supplier negotiations, just finding underpriced inventory.

## How the Math Works

Profit comes from the gap between what you pay in-store and what the item sells for on Amazon, minus Amazon's referral fee, FBA fulfillment fee, and your own time cost. Clearance finds at 70-90% off retail are where the real margin lives — full-price reselling rarely clears Amazon's fees.

## Can It Really Make You Money?

Yes, but it's inventory-dependent and time-intensive: profit comes from finding and buying deals consistently, not from a single big score. Sellers who treat it seriously (tracking price history, checking sales rank before buying, reinvesting profit into bigger buys) do better than those treating it as a side hobby.

## The Risks to Know Before You Start

- **Restricted brands and categories** — some require approval before you can sell them on Amazon.
- **Price and rank volatility** — an item that looked profitable last week can drop in price once more sellers list it.
- **Storage and long-term fees** — FBA inventory that doesn't sell quickly racks up storage costs that eat into margin.

## Scaling Beyond a Side Hustle

Retail arbitrage caps out fast if you're sourcing and listing manually. Sellers who scale usually diversify into wholesale or multiple sourcing channels, and sell across more than just Amazon.
