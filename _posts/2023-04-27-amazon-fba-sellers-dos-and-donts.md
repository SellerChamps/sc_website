## Do: Check Sales Rank and Fees Before You Buy Inventory

A low purchase price means nothing if Amazon's referral and FBA fulfillment fees eat your whole margin. Run the numbers, including storage fees, before committing to a product.

## Don't: Ignore Restricted Categories and Brands

Some categories and brands require upfront approval to sell on Amazon. Buying inventory you can't actually list is a fast way to tie up cash in unsellable stock.

## Do: Keep Your Inventory Performance Index (IPI) Healthy

Amazon tracks excess inventory, sell-through rate and stranded inventory through your IPI score — a low score limits your FBA storage capacity. Don't over-order stock you can't move at a healthy pace.

## Don't: Let Listings Sit with Incomplete Information

Missing bullet points, weak images or incomplete backend keywords hurt both conversion rate and search visibility — treat every listing as a first impression.

## Do: Monitor for Listing Hijackers and Price Erosion

Check your listings regularly for unauthorized sellers undercutting your price or piggybacking on your listing with inferior product — both hurt your Buy Box share.

## Don't: Rely on a Single Supplier or Single Channel

A sole-sourced product or a business that only sells on Amazon is one account suspension or one supplier issue away from zero revenue. Diversify where you can.

## Keep FBA Running Smoothly Across Every Channel

The sellers who scale past FBA basics are usually the ones also selling on eBay, Walmart or Shopify with the same inventory — without a system, that multiplies the inventory-tracking risk above. SellerChamp keeps stock synced in real time across every channel, so an Amazon sale updates your eBay and Walmart counts automatically.
