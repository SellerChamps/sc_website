Understanding where buyers actually shop — and how they behave once they're there — shapes which channels deserve your time and budget. Here's what matters most for multi-channel sellers.

## Marketplace Share of Online Shopping

Amazon remains the dominant starting point for product searches among online shoppers in the US, well ahead of search engines for pure product discovery — which is why strong Amazon listings matter even for sellers who want to grow elsewhere.

## Mobile Shopping Keeps Growing

A majority of marketplace browsing now happens on mobile devices, even when the purchase itself sometimes completes on desktop. Listings and storefronts that aren't mobile-friendly lose conversions regardless of how strong the product is.

## Multi-Channel Sellers Outperform Single-Channel Sellers

Sellers active on more than one marketplace consistently report higher total revenue than those relying on a single channel — diversifying exposure reduces the risk of any one platform's algorithm change or policy shift tanking your whole business.

## Reviews Directly Drive Conversion

Listings with a healthy volume of recent, positive reviews convert meaningfully better than comparable listings with few or no reviews — actively encouraging reviews post-purchase is one of the highest-leverage, lowest-cost tactics available.

## Fast Shipping Expectations Keep Rising

Buyer expectations around delivery speed continue to climb across every major marketplace — slower fulfillment measurably increases cart abandonment and return rates.

## What This Means for Your Strategy

The data points in one direction: being present on more than one channel, with fast fulfillment and an active review strategy, consistently outperforms a single-channel, hands-off approach.
