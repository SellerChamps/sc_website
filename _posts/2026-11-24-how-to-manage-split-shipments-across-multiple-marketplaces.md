<p>Split shipments happen when a single customer order gets fulfilled from more than one location, box, or carrier — and once you sell across Amazon, Walmart, eBay, and Shopify at the same time in 2026, they stop being the exception and become a weekly operational headache.</p>

<p>A split shipment isn&#39;t just two boxes instead of one. It&#39;s two tracking numbers, two delivery windows, and — on Amazon specifically — two chances for a late-shipment or missing-tracking defect to hit your account health score.</p>
<p>Sellers running inventory across five or more warehouses or 3PLs in 2026 see split shipments on roughly 15-30% of multi-item orders, depending on SKU spread. Miss the tracking update window on even one leg of that shipment and Amazon or Walmart can flag the order as unfulfilled, even though the product already left your dock.</p>
<p>The fix isn&#39;t hiring someone to babysit spreadsheets. It&#39;s building a repeatable process for splitting, tracking, and confirming shipments the same way every time, regardless of which marketplace the order came from.</p>
<h2>What you&#39;ll need</h2>
<ul>
<li>Access to seller accounts on every marketplace you split-ship from (Amazon Seller Central, Walmart Seller Center, eBay, Shopify admin, Etsy)</li>
<li>A multichannel order management tool — <a href="https://sellerchamp.com/">SellerChamp</a> centralizes orders, inventory counts, and tracking uploads from one dashboard instead of five separate logins</li>
<li>Carrier accounts with API or CSV tracking export capability (UPS, USPS, FedEx)</li>
<li>A defined SKU-to-warehouse mapping so you know in advance which locations can fulfill which items</li>
<li>30-45 minutes to set up split-shipment rules the first time; under 5 minutes per order after that</li>
</ul>
<h2>The steps</h2>
<h3>1. Map every SKU to its fulfillment source</h3>
<p>Before you can manage a split shipment, you need to know which warehouse, 3PL, or FBA location actually holds each SKU. Without this, splits happen by accident instead of by design.</p>
<p>Build a simple table: SKU, primary location, backup location, average pick time. Update it monthly as stock shifts between locations. Skipping this step is the single biggest reason sellers end up shipping partial orders they didn&#39;t plan to split.</p>
<p><strong>Common mistake:</strong> relying on marketplace inventory feeds alone. Amazon&#39;s FBA inventory count and your actual warehouse count can drift by 3-7% during high-volume weeks, which triggers unplanned splits.</p>
<h3>2. Set split-shipment rules per marketplace</h3>
<p>Amazon, Walmart, and eBay each have different tolerances for split shipments. Amazon expects tracking within 24-48 hours of the ship-by date per package, not per order. Walmart&#39;s WFS program flags orders that go over 2 business days without a tracking scan.</p>
<p>Configure rules in your order management system so orders route automatically: single-location orders ship whole, multi-location orders trigger a split flag before the pick ticket even prints. This is where a platform like SellerChamp earns its keep — order routing rules apply across every connected marketplace instead of being rebuilt channel by channel.</p>
<h3>3. Generate separate pick tickets for each shipment leg</h3>
<p>Once an order is flagged as split, generate individual pick tickets per warehouse or fulfillment location. Each ticket should carry its own packing slip referencing the original order number, not a duplicate order ID — marketplaces reconcile shipments against the original order, and mismatched IDs cause &quot;item not received&quot; disputes.</p>
<p><strong>Expected outcome:</strong> two (or more) discrete, trackable packages that both map back to one customer order in the marketplace backend.</p>
<h3>4. Assign and upload tracking numbers immediately</h3>
<p>This is the step sellers skip when volume spikes, and it&#39;s the one that costs the most. Every leg of a split shipment needs its own tracking number uploaded to the originating marketplace within the carrier&#39;s scan window — same-day for Walmart, within 48 hours for Amazon on most categories.</p>
<p>Batch tracking uploads once daily rather than order-by-order to avoid hitting Amazon&#39;s bulk-upload rate limits, which cap at a set number of tracking updates per hour on standard seller accounts.</p>
<p><strong>Common mistake:</strong> uploading one tracking number against the full order quantity instead of the partial quantity actually shipped. This closes the order prematurely and leaves the second package&#39;s buyer with no visible tracking.</p>
<h3>5. Update inventory counts across all channels the moment stock leaves</h3>
<p>A split shipment touches inventory at two locations, so both need to decrement in real time. If Amazon FBA ships 3 of 5 units and your Shopify warehouse ships the other 2, both counts need to reflect the sale immediately — otherwise you oversell the remaining stock on a third channel within the hour.</p>
<p>SellerChamp syncs inventory across connected marketplaces the moment a sale posts, so a partial fulfillment from one location updates available stock everywhere else without a manual recount.</p>
<h3>6. Confirm delivery on both legs before closing the order internally</h3>
<p>Don&#39;t mark an order &quot;complete&quot; in your internal system until both tracking numbers show delivered status. Customer service tickets spike when one package arrives and the second is still in transit — reps need visibility into both legs, not just the marketplace&#39;s single order status.</p>
<h3>7. Reconcile split-shipment costs weekly</h3>
<p>Split shipments cost more per order — two boxes, two labels, sometimes two carriers. Pull a weekly report on split-shipment frequency by SKU and location. If one SKU triggers splits on more than 20% of its orders, that&#39;s a signal to consolidate stock into a single location instead of absorbing the extra shipping cost order after order.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Tracking upload rejected on Amazon:</strong> Check that the tracking number is assigned to the correct partial quantity, not the full order quantity. A quantity mismatch is the most common rejection cause.</li>
<li><strong>Walmart order shows &quot;unfulfilled&quot; despite shipping:</strong> WFS requires a carrier scan within 2 business days. If the carrier hasn&#39;t scanned the package yet, the marketplace status won&#39;t update even with a valid tracking number entered.</li>
<li><strong>Inventory oversold across channels after a split:</strong> Real-time sync failed or lagged. Check your connection status on each marketplace integration and force a manual resync rather than waiting for the next scheduled pull.</li>
<li><strong>Customer disputes a missing item from a two-box order:</strong> Pull both tracking numbers and delivery confirmations before responding. Marketplaces side with sellers who can produce two distinct proof-of-delivery records tied to one order ID.</li>
<li><strong>Split shipments spiking on one SKU:</strong> Consolidate that SKU into a single fulfillment location if order volume allows — the shipping cost savings usually outweigh the flexibility of dual-location stock.</li>
<li><strong>Pick tickets printing with duplicate order numbers:</strong> Fix the pick-ticket template so both legs reference the original order ID with a suffix (like -A and -B) instead of generating two separate order records.</li>
</ul>

<li>A weekly split-shipment cost report, even a basic spreadsheet, to flag SKUs worth consolidating</li>
<li>Marketplace-specific seller policy pages for current shipment-window requirements, since Amazon and Walmart update fulfillment SLAs periodically</li>
</ul>
